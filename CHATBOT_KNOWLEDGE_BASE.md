## TRYING TO CONCEIVE (TTC) PHASE

### Understanding Your Menstrual Cycle

**Cycle Length and Ovulation:**
- A typical menstrual cycle is 28 days, but can range from 21 to 35 days (ACOG)
- Ovulation typically occurs 14 days before the start of your next period
- The fertile window is approximately 5 days before ovulation and the day of ovulation (Johns Hopkins Medicine)
- Nearly all pregnancies can be attributed to intercourse during a six-day period ending on the day of ovulation (NIH Research)

**What Happens During Your Cycle:**
- Days 1-5: Menstruation (shedding of uterine lining)
- Days 6-12: Follicular phase (estrogen levels rise, preparing egg for release)
- Days 13-14: Ovulation (egg is released from ovary)
- Days 15-28: Luteal phase (progesterone levels rise, preparing uterus for pregnancy)

### Calculating Your Fertile Window

**Standard Days Method (ACOG):**
- If your cycle is between 26-32 days long, days 8-19 are considered the most fertile days
- This method is based on fertility awareness research and has been validated by ACOG

**Calendar-Based Calculation:**
1. Track your menstrual cycle for at least 3 months
2. Identify the shortest and longest cycles
3. Subtract 18 from the shortest cycle to find the first fertile day
4. Subtract 11 from the longest cycle to find the last fertile day
5. Example: If shortest cycle is 26 days and longest is 32 days, fertile window is days 8-21

### Best Intercourse Timing for Conception

**Optimal Timing (ACOG & NIH Research):**
- Greatest chance of conception occurs with intercourse 1-2 days BEFORE ovulation
- Pregnancy may be achieved at any point within the fertile window
- Sperm can survive in the female reproductive tract for 3-5 days
- An egg survives for approximately 12-24 hours after being released

**Recommended Frequency:**
- Having intercourse every 2-3 days throughout your cycle ensures sperm is present during the fertile window
- Daily intercourse is not necessary and may reduce sperm count

### Ovulation Prediction and Testing Methods

**Ovulation Predictor Kits (OPKs) - ACOG:**
- OPKs detect the luteinizing hormone (LH) surge in urine
- LH surge occurs 24-36 hours before ovulation
- Most accurate when used in the morning (second urine of the day)
- Positive result indicates ovulation will likely occur within 24-36 hours
- Detection of LH surge is very sensitive and specific for ovulation (NIH)

**Basal Body Temperature (BBT) - ACOG:**
- BBT increases slightly (typically less than 0.5°F or 0.3°C) after ovulation
- Must be taken immediately upon waking, before getting out of bed
- Confirms ovulation has occurred (not predictive)
- Requires consistent tracking for 3+ months to identify patterns

**Cervical Mucus Method - ACOG:**
- Cervical mucus changes throughout the cycle
- Before ovulation: cervical mucus becomes clear, stretchy, and slippery (like egg white)
- This consistency helps sperm swim to the egg
- After ovulation: mucus becomes thick and sticky again
- Observe changes daily and record for pattern identification

**Fertility Tracking Apps:**
- ACOG notes that cycle-tracking apps based on cycle length alone cannot accurately predict ovulation
- Apps are most effective when combined with other tracking methods (OPKs, BBT, cervical mucus)
- Use apps as a tool to record and track patterns, not as sole predictor

**Progesterone Testing - ACOG:**
- Blood test measuring progesterone levels (typically done 7 days after ovulation)
- Confirms ovulation has occurred
- Not used to predict ovulation, only to confirm it happened

### Lifestyle Factors Affecting Fertility

**Stress and Fertility - NIH Research:**
- Chronic stress can affect hormone levels and ovulation
- Stress management techniques may help optimize fertility
- Consider yoga, meditation, exercise, or counseling

**Weight and Fertility - ACOG:**
- Both underweight and overweight conditions can affect fertility
- Maintaining a healthy BMI (18.5-24.9) may improve fertility outcomes
- Even modest weight loss (5-10%) can improve ovulation in overweight women

**Exercise and Fertility - ACOG & CDC:**
- Moderate-intensity exercise (150 minutes per week) supports fertility
- Excessive high-intensity exercise may disrupt ovulation
- Balance is key: regular moderate exercise is beneficial

**Nutrition for Fertility:**
- Eat a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins
- Include sources of folate (leafy greens, legumes) - important for egg quality
- Limit caffeine to less than 200mg per day
- Avoid smoking and excessive alcohol

### When to Seek Help

**ACOG Recommendations for Fertility Evaluation:**
- Women under 35: After 1 year of unprotected intercourse without conception
- Women 35 and older: After 6 months of unprotected intercourse without conception
- Women with irregular cycles or known fertility issues: Sooner evaluation recommended
- Men: After 1 year of unprotected intercourse without conception

## FIRST TRIMESTER (Weeks 1-13)

### Physical Changes

**Hormonal Changes:**
- Progesterone levels increase to support pregnancy
- Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) levels rise, detected by pregnancy tests
- Estrogen levels increase gradually

**Common Physical Changes:**
- Breast tenderness and enlargement
- Nausea and vomiting (morning sickness) - affects 70-80% of pregnant women (ACOG)
- Fatigue and increased need for sleep
- Frequent urination
- Food cravings and aversions
- Mood changes

### Safe Exercise During First Trimester (ACOG)

**General Guidelines:**
- 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week is recommended
- Continue pre-pregnancy exercise routine if previously active
- Avoid contact sports and activities with fall risk
- Stay hydrated and avoid overheating

**Safe Exercises:**
- Walking
- Swimming and water aerobics (excellent low-impact option)
- Stationary cycling
- Modified strength training
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels)
- Prenatal yoga

**Exercises to Avoid:**
- Contact sports
- Activities with high fall risk (skiing, horseback riding)
- Exercises lying flat on back after first trimester
- Scuba diving
- Extreme heat exposure (hot yoga, saunas)

### Nutrition in First Trimester

**Prenatal Vitamins:**
- Start prenatal vitamin with 400 micrograms of folic acid (ACOG)
- Folic acid reduces risk of neural tube defects
- Begin before conception if possible

**Key Nutrients:**
- Iron: 27 mg daily (increased from non-pregnant requirement)
- Calcium: 1000 mg daily
- Protein: 71 grams daily
- DHA (omega-3): 200-300 mg daily for fetal brain development

**Foods to Avoid:**
- Raw or undercooked meat, poultry, eggs
- Unpasteurized dairy products
- Raw fish and shellfish
- High-mercury fish (shark, swordfish, king mackerel)
- Unwashed produce
- Deli meats and hot dogs (unless heated until steaming)

### Warning Signs in First Trimester

**Contact Your Healthcare Provider Immediately If You Experience:**
- Severe abdominal pain
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Persistent severe nausea/vomiting (unable to keep food down)
- Severe headache
- Vision changes
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Fainting or severe dizziness

## SECOND TRIMESTER (Weeks 14-20)

### Physical Changes

**Common Changes:**
- "Showing" becomes more visible
- Morning sickness typically improves
- Energy levels often increase
- Skin changes (may develop dark line on abdomen - linea nigra)
- Hair and nail growth may increase
- Possible leg cramps and swelling

### Safe Exercise During Second Trimester (ACOG)

**General Guidelines:**
- Continue 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week
- Avoid exercises lying flat on back (may reduce blood flow to baby)
- Modify high-impact activities
- Pelvic floor exercises remain important

**Safe Exercises:**
- Walking
- Swimming and water aerobics (excellent choice)
- Stationary cycling
- Modified strength training (avoid heavy weights)
- Prenatal yoga
- Elliptical machine

**Modifications:**
- Avoid abdominal exercises that strain the growing belly
- Use a pregnancy pillow for support
- Modify positions as belly grows

### Prenatal Care

**Screening Tests (ACOG):**
- Quad screen or cell-free DNA testing (around 15-20 weeks)
- Glucose screening test (around 24-28 weeks)
- Ultrasound to check fetal development and anatomy

### Nutrition in Second Trimester

**Increased Caloric Needs:**
- Add approximately 300 calories per day to pre-pregnancy diet
- Focus on nutrient-dense foods, not empty calories

**Key Focus Areas:**
- Continue prenatal vitamins
- Increase iron intake (may need supplementation)
- Ensure adequate calcium for fetal bone development
- Include DHA for fetal brain development

## THIRD TRIMESTER (Weeks 21-40)

### Physical Changes

**Common Changes:**
- Significant weight gain (typically 25-35 pounds total)
- Increased swelling in feet and ankles
- Back pain and pelvic pressure
- Braxton-Hicks contractions (practice contractions)
- Difficulty sleeping
- Increased urinary frequency
- Possible gestational diabetes symptoms

### Safe Exercise During Third Trimester (ACOG)

**General Guidelines:**
- Continue 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week
- Focus on pelvic floor strengthening
- Avoid exercises that increase fall risk
- Listen to your body and reduce intensity as needed

**Safe Exercises:**
- Walking (excellent choice, low-impact)
- Swimming and water aerobics (reduces strain on joints)
- Stationary cycling
- Modified strength training
- Prenatal yoga and stretching
- Pelvic floor exercises (very important for labor preparation)

**Exercises to Avoid:**
- Contact sports
- High-impact activities
- Exercises lying flat on back
- Heavy lifting
- Activities with fall risk

### Preparation for Labor

**Pelvic Floor Exercises (Kegels):**
- Strengthen muscles that support bladder, bowel, and uterus
- Perform 10-20 repetitions, 3 times daily
- Hold each contraction for 3 seconds
- Benefits: easier labor, faster recovery, better bladder control

**Breathing Techniques:**
- Practice slow, deep breathing for labor
- Helps manage pain and stay calm

**Birth Plan:**
- Discuss preferences with healthcare provider
- Consider pain management options
- Plan for labor support (partner, doula, etc.)

### Warning Signs in Third Trimester

**Contact Healthcare Provider Immediately If You Experience:**
- Vaginal bleeding
- Severe abdominal pain
- Persistent contractions (every 5 minutes for 1 hour)
- Fluid leaking from vagina
- Severe headache
- Vision changes
- Chest pain
- Difficulty breathing
- Severe swelling or sudden weight gain

## POSTPARTUM PERIOD (After Birth)

### Physical Recovery Timeline (ACOG)

**First 6 Weeks (Vaginal Delivery):**
- Vaginal bleeding (lochia) gradually decreases
- Uterus returns to pre-pregnancy size
- Perineal soreness or pain (if tearing or episiotomy)
- Possible hemorrhoids
- Breast engorgement if breastfeeding

**First 8-12 Weeks (Cesarean Delivery):**
- Longer recovery due to surgical incision
- Pain at incision site
- Restricted activity longer than vaginal delivery
- Risk of blood clots slightly higher

### Postpartum Exercise Guidelines (ACOG)

**Timing:**
- Vaginal delivery: Can resume gentle exercise after 6 weeks (with healthcare provider approval)
- Cesarean delivery: Wait 8-12 weeks before resuming exercise
- Listen to your body and progress gradually

**First 6 Weeks (Gentle Recovery):**
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) can begin immediately
- Walking (short distances, gradually increasing)
- Gentle stretching
- Avoid heavy lifting and strenuous activity

**After 6-8 Weeks (Gradual Return):**
- Walking (30 minutes, 5 days per week)
- Swimming (excellent low-impact option)
- Stationary cycling
- Postpartum-specific exercises
- Modified strength training

**After 12 Weeks:**
- Gradually return to pre-pregnancy exercise routine
- Continue pelvic floor exercises
- Can resume running and high-impact activities (if cleared by provider)
- Strength training can increase gradually

**Exercises to Avoid Initially:**
- Heavy lifting (more than 15 pounds)
- High-impact activities (running, jumping)
- Abdominal exercises (until cleared by provider)
- Contact sports

### Postpartum Nutrition

**If Breastfeeding:**
- Add 300-500 calories per day to pre-pregnancy diet
- Continue prenatal vitamins
- Ensure adequate hydration (drink water with each feeding)
- Include foods rich in iron, calcium, and protein

**General Nutrition:**
- Eat balanced meals with protein, whole grains, fruits, and vegetables
- Include foods to support energy and mood
- Avoid restrictive dieting while recovering

### Postpartum Mental Health (ACOG)

**Baby Blues (First 2 Weeks):**
- Mood swings, anxiety, sadness
- Usually resolve without treatment
- Support from family and friends helpful

**Postpartum Depression (Beyond 2 Weeks):**
- Persistent sadness, anxiety, or hopelessness
- Loss of interest in activities
- Sleep disturbances (beyond normal newborn schedule)
- Difficulty bonding with baby
- Requires professional treatment

**Postpartum Anxiety:**
- Persistent worry and panic
- Racing thoughts
- Physical symptoms (heart palpitations, sweating)
- Requires professional support

**When to Seek Help:**
- Symptoms persist beyond 2 weeks
- Thoughts of harming yourself or baby
- Inability to care for yourself or baby
- Contact healthcare provider immediately

### Return to Intercourse (ACOG)

**Timing:**
- Wait until vaginal bleeding has stopped
- Typically 4-6 weeks after delivery
- Cesarean delivery: Wait 6-8 weeks
- Ensure emotional readiness

**Considerations:**
- Use contraception to prevent unintended pregnancy
- Breastfeeding does not prevent pregnancy
- May experience discomfort initially (use lubricant)
- Pelvic floor exercises support sexual function

## GENERAL PREGNANCY INFORMATION

### Prenatal Care Schedule (ACOG)

**First Trimester:**
- Initial visit: Comprehensive history, physical exam, dating ultrasound
- Monthly visits: Monitor weight, blood pressure, urine, fetal heart rate

**Second Trimester:**
- Monthly visits continue
- Anatomy ultrasound (18-20 weeks)
- Screening tests (15-20 weeks)

**Third Trimester:**
- Visits increase to every 2 weeks after 28 weeks
- Weekly visits after 36 weeks
- Monitor for signs of labor

### Medications During Pregnancy (ACOG)

**Generally Safe:**
- Prenatal vitamins with folic acid
- Acetaminophen for pain/fever
- Most antibiotics (penicillin-based)
- Antacids (calcium-based)
- Prenatal vitamins

**Avoid:**
- ACE inhibitors (blood pressure medication)
- Retinoids (acne medication)
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen) - especially third trimester
- Certain antibiotics (tetracyclines)
- Always consult healthcare provider before taking any medication

### Travel During Pregnancy (ACOG)

**Best Time to Travel:**
- Second trimester (weeks 14-20) is safest for travel
- Avoid first trimester (miscarriage risk) and third trimester (labor risk)

**Air Travel:**
- Generally safe until 36 weeks
- Wear compression socks to prevent blood clots
- Get up and walk every 2 hours
- Stay hydrated

**Car Travel:**
- Wear seatbelt low across hips
- Take breaks every 2 hours
- Avoid long road trips in third trimester

### Sexual Activity During Pregnancy (ACOG)

**Generally Safe:**
- Sexual activity is safe throughout pregnancy if low-risk pregnancy
- No increased risk of miscarriage from sexual activity
- Orgasm may cause Braxton-Hicks contractions (normal)

**When to Avoid:**
- Placenta previa or abruption
- Preterm labor risk
- Vaginal bleeding
- Rupture of membranes
- Consult healthcare provider if high-risk pregnancy

### Caffeine During Pregnancy (ACOG)

**Recommendation:**
- Limit caffeine to less than 200 mg per day
- Excessive caffeine may increase miscarriage risk
- Sources: coffee (95-200 mg per cup), tea (25-50 mg), chocolate (5-10 mg)

### Alcohol During Pregnancy (ACOG)

**Recommendation:**
- No amount of alcohol is known to be safe during pregnancy
- Alcohol increases risk of fetal alcohol spectrum disorders
- Complete abstinence is recommended

### Smoking During Pregnancy (ACOG)

**Risks:**
- Increases risk of miscarriage, preterm birth, low birth weight
- Increases risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS)
- Secondhand smoke also harmful

**Recommendation:**
- Quit smoking before conception if possible
- Quit immediately if already pregnant
- Seek support and resources for smoking cessation

### Workplace Safety During Pregnancy (ACOG)

**General Principles:**
- Avoid prolonged standing (more than 4 hours)
- Avoid heavy lifting (more than 50 pounds)
- Avoid exposure to hazardous chemicals or radiation
- Ensure adequate breaks and seating
- Discuss accommodations with employer

### Gestational Diabetes (ACOG)

**Screening:**
- Glucose screening test typically performed at 24-28 weeks
- Affects 2-10% of pregnancies

**Management:**
- Dietary modifications
- Regular blood sugar monitoring
- Exercise as tolerated
- Insulin if needed
- Resolves after delivery in most cases

### Preeclampsia (ACOG)

**Warning Signs:**
- High blood pressure (140/90 or higher)
- Protein in urine
- Severe headache
- Vision changes
- Upper abdominal pain
- Swelling in face and hands

**Risk Factors:**
- First pregnancy
- History of preeclampsia
- Multiple pregnancy
- Obesity
- Diabetes

**Management:**
- Regular blood pressure monitoring
- Immediate medical attention if symptoms develop

### Gestational Hypertension (ACOG)

**Definition:**
- High blood pressure that develops during pregnancy
- Typically after 20 weeks

**Management:**
- Regular monitoring
- Lifestyle modifications
- Medication if needed
- May resolve after delivery

## EXERCISE INFORMATION BY PHASE

### Trying to Conceive Phase Exercises

**Pelvic Floor Exercises (Kegels):**
- Strengthens muscles supporting reproductive organs
- Improves blood flow to pelvic area
- 3 sets x 10-15 reps daily
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Squats:**
- Strengthens legs and glutes
- Improves overall fitness
- 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Difficulty: Beginner to Intermediate

**Lunges:**
- Targets legs and glutes
- Improves balance and stability
- 3 sets x 10 reps each leg
- Difficulty: Intermediate

**Plank Hold:**
- Core strengthening
- Improves posture
- 3 sets x 20-30 seconds
- Difficulty: Intermediate

**Stationary Cycling:**
- Low-impact cardiovascular exercise
- 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Swimming or Water Aerobics:**
- Full-body, low-impact exercise
- Excellent for cardiovascular health
- 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

### First Trimester Exercises

**Modified Pelvic Floor Exercises:**
- Continue strengthening
- 3 sets x 10-15 reps daily
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Modified Squats:**
- Adjust depth as needed
- 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Difficulty: Beginner to Intermediate

**Wall Push-ups:**
- Upper body strengthening
- Modify as needed
- 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Stationary Cycling:**
- Cardiovascular exercise
- 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Swimming:**
- Excellent low-impact option
- 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Prenatal Yoga:**
- Flexibility and relaxation
- 1 set x 30-45 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

### Second Trimester Exercises

**Pelvic Floor Exercises:**
- Continue strengthening
- 3 sets x 10-15 reps daily
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Modified Squats:**
- Avoid going too deep
- 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Difficulty: Beginner to Intermediate

**Glute Bridges:**
- Strengthen glutes and lower back
- 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Difficulty: Intermediate

**Wall Push-ups:**
- Upper body strengthening
- 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Modified Lunges:**
- Adjust depth as belly grows
- 3 sets x 10 reps each leg
- Difficulty: Intermediate

**Swimming:**
- Excellent low-impact option
- 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Stationary Cycling:**
- Cardiovascular exercise
- 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

### Third Trimester Exercises

**Pelvic Floor Exercises:**
- Very important for labor preparation
- 3 sets x 10-15 reps daily
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Walking:**
- Excellent low-impact exercise
- 30 minutes, 5 days per week
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Swimming:**
- Reduces strain on joints
- 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Modified Wall Push-ups:**
- Upper body maintenance
- 3 sets x 8-10 reps
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Stationary Cycling:**
- Cardiovascular exercise
- 1 set x 15-20 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

**Prenatal Yoga:**
- Flexibility and relaxation
- 1 set x 30-45 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

### Postpartum Exercises

**First 6 Weeks (Gentle Recovery):**
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels): 3 sets x 10-15 reps daily
- Walking: Short distances, gradually increasing
- Gentle stretching: 10-15 minutes daily

**After 6-8 Weeks:**
- Walking: 30 minutes, 5 days per week
- Swimming: 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Stationary cycling: 1 set x 20-30 minutes
- Modified strength training: 2-3 times per week

**After 12 Weeks:**
- Gradually return to pre-pregnancy routine
- Can resume running and high-impact activities (if cleared)
- Strength training: Increase gradually
- Continue pelvic floor exercises
