## Primary Sources

### ACOG (American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists)

**Committee Opinion 804: Physical Activity and Exercise During Pregnancy and the Postpartum Period (April 2020)**
- URL: https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2020/04/physical-activity-and-exercise-during-pregnancy-and-the-postpartum-period
- Key Recommendations:
  - At least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week during pregnancy and postpartum
  - Activity should be spread throughout the week
  - Women who were physically active before pregnancy can continue vigorous-intensity activities
  - Exercise is safe for women with uncomplicated pregnancies
  - Avoid supine position after 20 weeks of gestation
  - Stay well hydrated, wear loose-fitting clothing, avoid high heat and humidity

**Safe Exercises (ACOG-Studied and Found Safe):**
- Walking
- Stationary cycling
- Aerobic exercises
- Dancing
- Resistance exercises (weights, elastic bands)
- Stretching exercises
- Hydrotherapy, water aerobics

**Benefits of Exercise in Pregnancy:**
- Higher incidence of vaginal delivery
- Lower incidence of:
  - Excessive gestational weight gain
  - Gestational diabetes mellitus
  - Gestational hypertensive disorders
  - Preterm birth
  - Cesarean birth
  - Lower birth weight

**Fetal Response to Maternal Exercise:**
- Minimum to moderate increases in fetal heart rate (10-30 bpm over baseline)
- Minimal to no differences in birth weight
- Women exercising vigorously in third trimester may deliver infants 200-400g lighter (not fetal growth restriction)
- 30 minutes of strenuous exercise well tolerated by mother and fetus

**Postpartum Exercise:**
- Same 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity recommended
- Strength conditioning exercises recommended
- Women can continue pre-pregnancy exercise routines if they were physically active

## Exercise Recommendations by Trimester

### Trying to Conceive Phase

**ACOG Guidance:**
- Regular exercise improves overall health and fertility
- 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week
- Strength training 2-3 times per week
- Avoid excessive exercise that may interfere with fertility

**Safe Exercises:**
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) - improves blood flow to reproductive organs
- Squats - strengthens legs and core
- Lunges - improves balance and leg strength
- Plank holds - core strengthening
- Stationary cycling - low-impact cardio
- Swimming/water aerobics - full-body, low-impact

---

### First Trimester (Weeks 1-13)

**ACOG Guidance:**
- Most exercises are safe during first trimester
- Avoid contact sports
- Avoid exercises lying flat on back after 12 weeks
- Stay well hydrated, avoid overheating, especially in first trimester
- Temperature regulation is critical - avoid hot tubs, saunas during pregnancy

**Safe Exercises:**
- Modified squats - strengthens legs for pregnancy weight
- Wall push-ups - upper body strength without abdominal strain
- Stationary lunges - leg strength and balance
- Dumbbell rows (light weight) - back strengthening for posture
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) - prepares for labor
- Stationary cycling - low-impact cardio

**Precautions:**
- Do not do floor push-ups
- Avoid exercises that put pressure on abdomen
- Avoid lying flat on back for extended periods after 12 weeks

---

### Second Trimester (Weeks 14-27)

**ACOG Guidance:**
- "Honeymoon phase" - often more energy
- Avoid lying flat on back for extended periods (after 20 weeks)
- Aortocaval compression from gravid uterus can cause decreased venous return and hypotension
- Continue 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week

**Safe Exercises:**
- Glute bridges - strengthens glutes and lower back
- Side-lying leg lifts - hip stabilizers without back strain
- Modified planks (wall or incline) - core strength without abdominal pressure
- Stationary lunges (modified) - leg strength with modifications
- Dumbbell bicep curls (light) - maintains arm strength
- Swimming/water aerobics - relieves joint pressure, full-body workout

**Precautions:**
- Do not lie flat on back for extended periods after 20 weeks
- Avoid any exercise causing doming of the abdomen
- Do not do floor planks

---

### Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40)

**ACOG Guidance:**
- Exercise becomes gentler and more focused on preparation
- Recommended frequency: 3-4 days per week at gentle intensity
- Focus on pelvic floor strengthening and flexibility
- Continue aerobic activity but at reduced intensity
- Avoid strenuous exercise in third trimester (lighter weight babies noted but not growth restriction)

**Safe Exercises:**
- Modified squats (wall support) - opens pelvis, prepares for labor
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) - essential for labor preparation
- Walking (increased duration) - 45-60 minutes daily
- Prenatal yoga (gentle) - flexibility and nervous system calming
- Swimming/water aerobics - relieves back pain and joint pressure
- Side-lying leg lifts - hip stabilizers without back strain

**Precautions:**
- Do not squat too deeply
- Avoid vigorous exercise
- Stop if dizzy or uncomfortable
- Listen to body signals

---

### Postpartum Recovery

**ACOG Guidance:**
- Wait for medical clearance before starting exercise (typically 6 weeks for vaginal delivery, 8-12 weeks for C-section)
- Same 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity recommended once cleared
- Strength conditioning exercises recommended
- Physical activity can prevent postpartum depression

**Safe Exercises (After Medical Clearance):**
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) - rebuilds pelvic floor strength
- Abdominal bracing - gentle core strengthening
- Glute bridges - strengthens glutes and lower back
- Walking - low-impact cardio
- Modified planks - core strengthening
- Swimming/water aerobics - full-body, low-impact

**Special Considerations:**
- Vaginal delivery: Start with very gentle exercises, increase gradually
- C-section: Abdominal incision needs time to heal, avoid heavy lifting initially
- Diastasis recti: Avoid sit-ups and crunches, work with physical therapist if severe
- Breastfeeding: Exercise does not affect milk, wear supportive sports bra, stay hydrated

## Warning Signs to Stop Exercise

**ACOG Guidelines - Stop Exercise and Contact Provider If:**
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leakage
- Severe abdominal or pelvic pain
- Persistent contractions (more than 6 per hour in third trimester)
- Chest pain or severe shortness of breath
- Dizziness, fainting, or vision changes
- Decreased fetal movement
- Increased vaginal bleeding or clots (postpartum)
- Incision reopening or increased drainage (postpartum)

## Nutrition Guidelines

### General Pregnancy Nutrition (ACOG)

**Key Nutrients:**
- Protein: Essential for fetal development
- Iron: Prevents anemia, replaces blood loss
- Calcium: Bone development, especially if breastfeeding
- Omega-3 DHA: Brain development
- Vitamin D: Mood, bone health
- Fiber: Prevents constipation
- Fluids: Hydration, milk production

**Breastfeeding Nutrition:**
- Additional 500 calories per day needed
- Drink water every time you nurse
- Keep healthy snacks within reach

## Mental Health

### Postpartum Mental Health (ACOG)

**Baby Blues (Normal - up to 80% of mothers):**
- Mood swings
- Crying spells
- Anxiety
- Difficulty sleeping
- Resolves within 2 weeks

**Postpartum Depression/Anxiety (Requires Professional Support):**
- Persistent sadness or emptiness
- Difficulty bonding with baby
- Overwhelming anxiety or panic
- Thoughts of harming yourself or baby
- Symptoms lasting beyond 2 weeks

**Prevention:**
- Physical activity can be an essential factor in prevention of depressive disorders in postpartum period
- Professional support is essential if symptoms occur

## Skincare During Pregnancy

### Safe Ingredients (ACOG-Approved)

**First and Second Trimester Safe:**
- Vitamin C Serum - brightening, melasma
- Hyaluronic Acid - deep hydration
- Azelaic Acid - melasma, acne, safe for pregnancy pigmentation
- Niacinamide - barrier repair, pregnancy glow
- Mineral SPF 50+ - melasma prevention
- Cocoa/Shea Butter - stretch mark prevention

### Avoid During Pregnancy

**Not Recommended:**
- Retinoids - birth defect risk (can reintroduce after weaning)
- Hydroquinone - high absorption (can reintroduce after weaning with MD approval)
- Chemical Peels - deep penetration (wait until postpartum)
- Laser Treatments - not studied in pregnancy (wait until postpartum)

---

## Contraindications to Exercise

**ACOG States - Few maternal medical conditions absolutely contraindicate aerobic exercise:**
- Consultation with specialists advised when questions exist
- Exercise regimens should be individualized for women with comorbidities
- Activity restriction should NOT be prescribed routinely to reduce preterm birth

## Additional Scientific Sources

### CDC Guidelines

**CDC: Pregnant & Postpartum Activity - An Overview (Updated Dec 4, 2025)**
- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/healthy-pregnant-or-postpartum-women.html
- Key Points:
  - Safe for healthy pregnant and postpartum women to do moderate-intensity physical activity
  - 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (30 minutes, 5 days a week)
  - Reduces risk of excessive weight gain during pregnancy
  - Reduces risk of gestational diabetes during pregnancy
  - Reduces symptoms of postpartum depression
  - Women who already do vigorous-intensity aerobic activity (running) can continue during pregnancy
  - After first trimester, avoid activities requiring lying flat on back

### Stress and Fertility

**NIH Systematic Review: "Infertility and cortisol: a systematic review" (2023)**
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10344356/
- Key Finding: In infertile women, cortisol levels during and post-ovulation are positively correlated with anxiety symptoms
- Source: NIH/PubMed Central

**Research on Stress and Fertility**
- Studies show relationship between high perceived stress levels and elevated cortisol
- Elevated cortisol can suppress GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) from hypothalamus
- This leads to reduction in LH (luteinizing hormone) and FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)
- However, most women under stress still conceive if ovulation occurs regularly

### Exercise and Ovulation

**PubMed Systematic Review: "Effect of Exercise on Ovulation"**
- Key Finding: Increased risk of anovulation (lack of ovulation) in extremely heavy exercisers (>60 min/day)
- Vigorous exercise of 30-60 min/day is safe and does not disrupt ovulation
- Very low body fat from excessive exercise can lead to hormonal imbalances
- Overtraining can negatively impact ovulation

**NIH Research: "The effect of physical activity on fertility: a mini-review"**
- Key Finding: Detrimental effects of vigorous exercise on fertility in healthy-weight women might be countered through increased calorie intake
- Moderate exercise (30-60 min/day) improves fertility outcomes
- Extreme exercise (>60 min/day) may negatively impact fertility

### Postpartum Mental Health

**ACOG & CDC Research**
- Physical activity is an essential factor in prevention of postpartum depression
- Exercise combined with healthy eating helps with weight loss after delivery
- Benefits extend throughout the first year postpartum
